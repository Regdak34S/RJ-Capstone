# Candidate C - Content Creation Workflow and Drafting App



## 1. Problem
**What real problem does this address?**  
A problem I see in content creation is moving from the first draft to a finished piece of content. Creating content is not just about having an idea. I also have to write, revise, organize, review, and prepare the final content. Without a clear workflow, it can be easy to lose track of what stage the project is in.

**Who experiences the problem?**  
Content creators and students who create digital content can experience this problem. It especially relates to me because I work on digital projects and use tools such as ChatGPT, video tools, and other software during the creation process.

**What situation causes the problem?**  
The problem happens when a project has several stages and I do not have one clear way to track them. For example, I may have an idea and a draft but not know whether I have completed the research, revision, asset collection, or final preparation.

**What goes wrong?**  
I may go back and redo work that I already completed or forget an important part of the project. This can make the final content take longer to complete.

## 2. Cost and current behavior
**What does the problem cost, using a real number?**  
The cost would be the amount of time I spend going back through a project to determine what is finished and what still needs work. My real number is 2-4 hours per content project, based on my own experience and time tracking.

**What do people currently do instead?**  
People can use checklists, notes, spreadsheets, folders, or general project-management applications. I can also keep track of the process mentally or use separate documents for different parts of the project.

**Why isn't that workaround good enough?**  
General tools are not necessarily designed around the specific steps involved in creating content. I may have to create my own checklist and manually keep it updated. This can become another task instead of helping me create the content.

## 3. Proposed project
**What would your project actually need to do?**  
The project would provide a simple workflow for creating content. A user could start a project, enter the basic idea, work through drafting and revision stages, track progress, and prepare the final content. It would show which parts are completed and which parts still need attention.

## 4. Difference
**What makes this idea different from the other two?**  
This idea focuses on the entire workflow from idea to finished content. Candidate A focuses mostly on organizing content, while Candidate B focuses on AI assistance. Candidate C would connect the different stages together and help me see the progress of the whole project.

## 5. Dependencies
**What outside services, APIs, software, or data would it depend on?**  
The basic version could be built using standard web-development tools and local storage, so it would not need many outside services. Optional AI functionality could be added later, but I would not make the project dependent on AI for its core function.

## 6. Feasibility
**What could make the idea impossible or too large for this course?**  
The main risk would be trying to build too many content-creation features. A full video editor, audio editor, cloud storage system, social media publishing system, or advanced AI system would be too large. I would need to keep the project focused on managing the workflow rather than actually replacing professional content-creation software.

## 7. Setup
**What is the best way to set up this idea to get ready for the project?**  
I would first map out the content-creation process I normally follow, from having an idea through completing the final content. I would then identify the most important stages and turn those stages into a simple workflow. I would create a small prototype using one real content project and see whether the workflow helps me understand what needs to be done next.
